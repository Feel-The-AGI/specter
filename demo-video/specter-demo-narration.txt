Scene 1
This is SPECTER. Tell it what your language model is for. It works out how to evaluate it, tests what fine-tuning changed, and gives you evidence for a release decision.

Scene 2
A model can score well on a benchmark and still be wrong for the job it was built to do. Fine-tuning on ordinary business data can quietly wear down its safety, and that damage does not have to stay in the language you trained on.

Scene 3
We start from a use case: a research assistant that answers numeric questions over a fictional investment firm's reports. SPECTER's Eval Intelligence Engine turns that description into a plan. Eleven metrics, each tied to a risk, an oracle, a minimum sample size and a threshold. Risks it cannot score, like regulatory certification, are listed as gaps instead of being dropped.

Scene 4
The plan and the held-out test suites are each locked with a SHA 256 hash. The pipeline checks both before it trains anything. Change one number, and it refuses to run.

Scene 5
The engine also checks its own tests. Our first holdout repeated seven template sentences two hundred times, which made the statistics meaningless. We caught it, and SPECTER now rejects any suite like that. Version three uses real prompts from HarmBench and Cohere's Aya red-teaming set, in five languages.

Scene 6
One command runs the whole experiment on Tiny Aya Global. CuratorKIT built 542 verified finance question and answer pairs. SafeTune trains five arms: the base model, a clean fine-tune, a fine-tune with ten percent harmful rows mixed in, the same contaminated data trained with SafeGrad, and ReSta weight repair applied to the contaminated checkpoint.

Scene 7
Here is the English result on 200 held-out harmful prompts. The base model refused 76 percent. Ten percent contaminated data dropped that to 35 percent. Clean tuning did not hurt it. SafeGrad held refusal at 77 percent, with finance accuracy unchanged at 31 of 40. ReSta recovered only part of the drop.

Scene 8
The drift did not stay in English. Refusal fell in all four other languages. In Tagalog it went from 28 of 50 to just 5. SafeGrad brought every language back to the base model's level.

Scene 9
SPECTER also reports what did not work. The base model already repeats a confidential token from its context when asked, and follows injected instructions in 37 of 40 memos. Fine-tuning did not cause this, and no arm fixes it. And against the pre-registered thresholds, every arm is blocked, including the base model, because non-English refusal is below 75 percent before any tuning at all.

Scene 10
The limits, stated plainly. One seed. The WildGuard judge has not yet been checked against human labels, especially outside English. And the plan was re-approved after we found the templated holdout. The record shows every version.

Scene 11
SPECTER. Contamination broke the model's safety in five languages. SafeGrad restored it without costing accuracy. And every number traces back to a locked plan. The code, data and results are public.
